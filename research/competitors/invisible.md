- Hero headline (quote, under 15 words): home "Advancing AI models from frontier innovation to ..." (cut off). The track pages are clearer: /ai-training "Where the world's top AI models are trained", /data-lab "We train the world's leading AI models", /about "Artificial intelligence. Real outcomes."

A home FAQ entry asks what an "applied AI research company" is. The footer describes AI support running from frontier model-building research to enterprise deployment.
- Primary audience(s): both, plus a third.

- One-sentence entity definition they repeat (paraphrase): Invisible says it trained the models the labs ship and is now taking that lab-grade training know-how into enterprise operations, covering AI "from genesis to the final mile".

## 4. Voice and tone
- Five adjectives: confident, polished, outcome-led, contrarian (aimed at consultants and slow transformation programs), aphoristic.
- Sentence style: short two-beat headings ("Complex tasks. Simple automation."), with "not X, but Y" and "no X, no Y" patterns. Body copy uses full, fairly short sentences. The FAQs are noticeably calmer and more explanatory than the headings.
- Person: "we" and "you", with frequent "your models" and "your operations".
- Jargon level and the technical terms they lean on: it depends on the track.
  - Lab pages are dense: RLHF, SFT, RL environments, reward design, reward shaping, rubrics, verifiers, trajectories, rollouts, reward hacking, red-teaming, curriculum learning, blind annotation.
  - Enterprise pages drop research words entirely in favor of exceptions, edge cases, routing, escalation, confidence, audit and source evidence.
  - House phrases: "services-to-software", "final mile", "mirror worlds".
- How they use numbers:
  - One very large share claim (80%+ of leading models).
  - Network sizes (24,000+ vetted experts on home, 22M+ "access to global experts" on the experts page, 1.5M+ STEM, 800+ domains, 80 to 110+ languages).
  - Percentage outcomes (70%, 80%, 99%).
  - Speed promises ("days, not months", "weeks to working AI").
  - Audience size (45,000+ subscribers).
  - Case-study numbers usually come without baselines, sample sizes or methods.
- Two or three paraphrased examples of typical lines (not verbatim copy):
  - Training is presented as an ongoing pipeline that keeps adapting as the model changes, not a one-off project.
  - The engagement starts with one workflow inside your systems and grows from there, instead of months of discovery decks.
  - Reward design matters too much to automate, so domain experts write the task logic and the rubrics.

## 6. How they earn trust without (or before) proof
Several solution pages (RL environments, back-office automation) have empty case-study slots. Here is what they lean on instead:
- **Mechanism detail.** On RL environments: experts define the task logic, write reward rubrics and annotate trajectories; every run is logged and replayable; tasks get harder step by step; expert-built reward logic plus replay logs guard against reward hacking.
- **Governance design for enterprise.** Low-confidence decisions go to a human reviewer at set checkpoints, high-stakes outputs are checked before the next step, agents show their source evidence, and workflows can be set up to meet audit needs.
- **Principles as commitments.** Model-agnostic, no vendor lock-in, data stays in place, transparency over black-box AI, and measuring results from the first day.
- **A numbered method.** Connect, train, validate, deploy and measure, with a named leader quoted on the step about connecting systems.
- **Teaching FAQs.** Every page ends with a deep FAQ that explains the category (what an RL environment is, AI vs RPA, how human oversight works).
- **Pedigree and volume.** Leadership résumés, awards, and a large content library that signals an active, informed firm.
- **Borrowed authority.** Citations in WEF and Meta publications.

## 9. Lab vs enterprise messaging
- **To labs: prestige and capacity.** The section heading "The preferred vendor for leading AI labs", research-grade quality, PhD-level experts, named techniques (RLHF, SFT, RL environments, red-teaming), counts of languages and domains, and benchmark scores and win rates as proof. It speaks to data and evaluation teams and treats training as continuous.
- **To enterprises: problems and outcomes.** "Automate back-office work full of exceptions", messy inputs, days to minutes, forward-deployed engineers, start with one workflow, your data stays in your systems, audit-ready, no lock-in, and ROI and hours saved. Research words are deliberately left out of these pages.
- **The bridge.** The lab record is used as the credential for enterprise buyers (we trained the models, now we make them work for you). The closing line about the best data appears on both tracks.
- **One gap.** Both audiences get the same "Book a demo" CTA, which suits enterprise buyers better than lab research teams.

## 10. What Ramanujan should borrow
1. **Two tracks under one entity line.** A "Labs" track (post-training data, evals, RL environments) and a "Business" track (fine-tuning, agents, datasets), each with its own vocabulary, joined by one sentence. Ramanujan can make the bridge about method, not track record: frontier-grade post-training methods applied to business problems. This is information architecture, so it needs no clients.
2. **A "How we work" page with numbered steps and stated principles.** For example: scope, data, train, evaluate, deploy and measure. Then commitments: model-agnostic, your data stays in your environment, you own the weights, datasets and evals, no lock-in, start with one workflow. These are promises Ramanujan can keep from day one, not claims about the past.
3. **Mechanism-level detail where proof is missing.** On each capability page, say who writes the rubrics, how verifiers check outputs, that runs are logged and replayable, and how reward hacking is caught. Invisible does exactly this on pages with no case study.
4. **Sober two-beat headlines around one bold line.** Keep "The Godfathers of Applied AI" as the single bold statement and put short, factual two-beat lines under it. The surrounding tone then reads corporate and calm.
5. **A teaching FAQ on every page, plus buyer-intent guides** (how to evaluate a fine-tuning partner, SFT vs preference tuning, a 90-day plan for a first fine-tune). This is honest authority and also strong for SEO/GEO.
6. **Human-in-the-loop governance language for agents.** Confidence thresholds, escalation to a person, source evidence and an audit trail. It describes the design, not a history of results.
7. **A trust page that states practices, not badges.** Encryption, access control, data retention and deletion on request, NDA by default, a named list of subprocessors, and a dated "where we are on SOC 2" line. Borrow the "Request access" pattern as "Request our security overview".

## 11. What Ramanujan should avoid
- **Market-share or lab-relationship claims.** Nothing like "trained X% of leading models" and no "preferred vendor for labs". Invisible's 80% claim could not be independently verified even though it is a large, established firm.
- **Large or shifting network numbers.** Invisible's expert counts run from 24,000 to 22M depending on the page. Ramanujan should show no expert counts until they are real and countable.
- **Ambiguous logo strips.** Cloud or model-provider logos (AWS, Microsoft, OpenAI, Anthropic) imply endorsement even when they are only vendors. Show none until there are real customers, and label them as customers.
- **Empty case-study slots or placeholder sections.** Remove a section rather than ship it blank.
- **Metrics without a baseline, sample size or method.** When Ramanujan publishes its own results, show the base model, the eval set, the number of tasks and how scoring works.
- **Headlines that claim more than the body.** One Invisible case-study headline says "outperforms" while the body says "matches or outperforms".
- **Codenamed "products" for services that are not software yet.** Five named products would read as invented for a new studio.
- **Badge-only trust pages and certifications not held.** Say "in progress" with a date, or say nothing.
- **Audience-size and award flexes** such as newsletter subscriber counts and pay-to-enter awards.
